# Newspaper composition for the existing blog

Reference: VnExpress's technology reporting, for example https://vnexpress.net/lo-hong-java-moi-lam-day-len-lo-ngai-ve-an-ninh-mang-5146330.html (inspected 2026-11-19). Consult a current accessible article when the writing context needs it. Borrow information hierarchy and reading rhythm; write original reporting/synthesis and retain the portfolio's dark visual identity.

1. **Headline**: state what changed and who is affected, without hype or unverified superlatives. Respect the current title limit.
2. **Sapo/excerpt**: one or two sentences summarizing the event and practical significance, within the editor excerpt limit. Attribute contested or vendor-provided claims.
3. **Opening**: the development, event date, availability and source. Give readers the essential fact before technical background.
4. **Body**: short paragraphs of one to three sentences; put qualifications next to the claim. Use useful subheadings when the story needs them, not a mandatory checklist of identical sections. Aim for roughly 450-800 English words for substantial news, shorter for a simple confirmed update; evidence determines length.
5. **Figures**: cover plus only useful inline images; each has a factual caption, creator/publisher credit and adjacent source/license link. Source diagrams are illustrations from their publisher, not photographs or independently verified results. Archival photographs say so. Do not overload a short story with decorative images.
6. **Context**: explain what developers can actually use, who has access, practical limitations and what remains unknown. Distinguish analysis from sourced facts. Avoid turning every news report into a tutorial or an opinion essay.
7. **Closing**: next milestone or concrete unresolved point, then compact linked primary sources. Avoid canned conclusions and promotional portfolio CTAs inside article prose.

Use supported editor nodes and marks. Plain paragraphs, h2/h3, figures, genuine brief quotations and lists where useful are enough. Never inject HTML or invent unsupported table/caption nodes. Preserve code samples, URLs and product identifiers; use ASCII hyphens for prose separators, per repository rules.

Before handoff, read the draft as a reader: can the event and significance be understood from the headline and sapo, are figures legible, are dates and access restrictions clear, and does every specific claim have evidence? A source URL must support the associated claim, not merely mention the same company.
